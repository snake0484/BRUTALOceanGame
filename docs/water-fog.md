# Water fog

Volumetric fog inside the water: the body of the ocean and of voxel water is the light scattered back along the eye's ray through the water it crosses, integrated voxel by voxel, not a tint picked by depth. Each water voxel carries a colour and a fog density; the march stops as soon as the fog has taken nearly all the light (the hard limit), so heavy fog is cheap and clear water is seen into as far as it is clear.

## Data

- **Per cell** (`WaterBlock.Colour`, `WaterColour`, RGBA8 in a `uint`): rgb is the water's scatter tint, a its fog density. Density 255 would be opaque within one voxel; 0 means unset (no water has ever been there). The sea is `WaterColour.Sea` (turquoise, density 32: 1/e over 2 m along the eye's ray at the default extinction of 4/m), the crater lake `WaterColour.Lake` (greener, denser), scene fills `WaterColour.Pond` (brown, turbid; `VoxelWaterSystem.Fill` takes a colour).
- **Where it comes from:** `WaterBlock.ReadSolids` gives held cells their body's colour (`WaterBodies.Body.Colour`; the sea's and the generated bodies' are set when they are added, a born body takes its seed cell's) and generated free water the sea's; a refresh keeps what the cells had. A pond poured by `Fill` blends its colour in by the mass it adds.
- **It travels with the water** (pipe model only, `WaterPipes.ApplyFluxes` → `Carried`): a cell's new colour is what stayed of its own water and what each face brought in, blended by mass (`WaterColour.Blend`), read from every neighbour's previous colour and written aside in a per-thread scratch, then landed on the block's wet rows. A dry cell takes the colour of what fills it; silt stays with its water. The halo planes carry the neighbours' colours (`WaterHalo.CopyColour`, each step). The automaton (`--water-automaton`) does not carry it: its cells keep whatever colour they were given, and a wet cell with no colour is read as sea water.
- **Cost:** 157 KB per block (80 MB for the pool of 512), 1 scratch array per worker thread; the apply pass does six max/min and, where water flows in, up to six blends per cell.

## The fog volume (`WaterFogVolume`, Engine)

- A 3D texture, RGBA8, 128 × 96 × 128 texels (6 MB), one per metre, **toroidal**: the texel of world cell (x, y, z) is (x mod 128, y mod 96, z mod 128), so a window of 4 × 3 × 4 blocks around the camera (the camera's block second from the low edge in x and z, in the middle in y; 128 m square, 96 m tall) needs only the blocks that enter it uploaded. Nearest filtering, repeat addressing.
- A texel is the fog of its voxel: rgb the tint, a the density scaled by the fill (held sea water at 1 + compression counts as full), **255 = solid**, which stops a march; 0 = air.
- **Filled per frame** (`VoxelWaterSystem.FillFog`): for each block of the window, the texture's copy is stale when its (block, revision) differs from the source's. A loaded sim block's revision (`WaterBlock.Revision`) moves on every step, activation, refresh, fill, refill, join or birth. A block no sim block covers is read from the bathymetry: the ground at the block's 18 × 18 grid cell centres once (`BathymetryGrid.SampleGround`, which at a centre is that cell's ground), bilinear per voxel column by hand so a beach slopes instead of stepping; per column the last solid cell, the part-wet cell over it and the top water cell, so each cell is three integer compares; solid under the ground, sea water from there up to the sea-level layer, air elsewhere. A column is sea when any grid cell within 3 (6 m) is (the mask dilated in two passes over the block's 24 × 24 footprint): the mask marks a cell sea only where its y = −1 voxel holds water, so the last half metre of a beach's slope under sea level and the run-up over it (several metres on a gentle beach) read as dry, and the first build drew a 1 m staircase of fog-less water along that line. Such a block is keyed on a hash of the ground and sea state of its footprint's cells (`BathymetryKey`, hashed for the window's 48 blocks whenever the grid's version moves, into the sign-bit half so it never reads as a sim block's revision), so a rebuild of the kilometre grid refills only the blocks whose own ground changed; the key must take the ground as the fill reads it, the top on the beach included, or a beach refined by streaming kept its coarse waterline. At most 8 blocks (1 MB) and 0.5 ms a frame are filled and copied, round-robin from where the last frame stopped, so stepping blocks never starve the rest; the cells are written to a managed scratch and copied once into the write-combined staging. Fill time is `Fog fill` in the profiler; the copies are the `Fog volume` GPU scope.
- **Uniforms** (shared by the terrain, ocean and voxel water sets): the window's origin, the size and sea level, the march parameters (extinction at density 1, the stop transmittance, the step, the step cap), the open sea's tint and density, and whether the camera is under water and the surface over it.

## The march (`water_fog.glsl`)

- `waterFogMarch(p, dir, maxDist, surfaceY, bedDepth, jitter, inscatter, t)`: from p along dir in steps (1 m), each step's texel multiplies the transmittance t by exp(−extinction × density × ds) and adds the texel's glow times the light lost. `fogGlow` = tint × (the light coming down through the surface, the sun's share and the sky's as `water_shading.glsl` lights the seabed, absorbed over the depth under surfaceY, × `FOG_GAIN_DOWN` 0.35; plus the light that reached the sand under the ray, bedDepth down, and bounced back up to the texel, absorbed both ways at `FOG_BED_ABSORB` (0.6, 0.3, 0.25)/m, × `FOG_GAIN_BED` 4), plus the deep glow `FOG_DEEP` so deep water is never black. The bed is taken as flat under the ray at the fragment's thickness (the terrain gives its own depth when the camera is under water). The two terms are what the depth shading did in one: the sand-lit turquoise of the shallows, gone a few metres down, over the dark blue of the deep; without the bed term the sea was either milky (gain for the shallows) or black (gain for the deep). The march ends:
  - at a **solid** texel: what was drawn there (the bed) shows through at t;
  - at the **hard limit**: t under the stop transmittance (0.02) counts the rest as fog, t = 0, and the march stops, so murky water costs a handful of steps however far the ray would go;
  - at maxDist (the fragment, for an underwater camera);
  - on leaving the window, from where the **open sea's closed form** (`fogSea`: uniform fog with the light falling off with depth along the ray, per channel) carries on below sea level.
- Each segment is read at one point a fixed fraction (`fogJitter`, per pixel) of the way along it, so a sloping bed is noise rather than contour bands, never flickers, and the path integrated is exact whatever the jitter (reading the first sample a fraction in and skipping that fraction made the underwater view grain).
- **Camera in the air** (`shadeWater` → `waterBodyFog`): the water surface's fragment marches into the water along the eye's ray refracted by the surface normal (`refract`, n = 1.333: one call a pixel, the path to the bed shortens and the fog reads with the ripples; the bed itself stays where the framebuffer has it, there being no colour buffer to look up at the refracted point) as far as the bed **the eye's ray meets** (`waterSceneBehind`: the opaque scene's depth copied before the water, `ScreenReflections`, which copies it whenever the fog is on even with the reflections off; the bed's drop under the surface point, then along the refracted ray as to a flat bed at that drop, so a flat bed reads as before). The ground straight under the fragment (its thickness) stands in where the scene has nothing behind it; taken everywhere, a slope fogged the water by the column under the surface point, not the water the pixel looks through, too deep looking up a beach and too shallow looking out over a drop-off. (The volume's solid texels, a metre coarse, only stop what the thickness can't know, a cave roof or a dug wall). A fragment whose texel is air with water in the cell under it (a crest over the volume's top water cell) first takes the sea's closed form down to that cell; one with air or the bed under it stands on water the volume doesn't hold (open sea past the mask's reach, the swash up a gentle beach; taking the bed's solid texel as water, black at density 1, drew a dark strip of 1 m steps along the shore), and one whose texel holds water of one colour as far as the ray goes (the texel at the fragment against one a couple of metres in, `fogSameWater`; the bed or air there counts as this water, since deciding by that sample's cell drew the mesh's triangles as a sawtooth of marched against closed-form water along the lake's shore) takes the closed form in that water's own tint and density (`fogUniform`; the sea's is `fogSea`) to the bed with no march at all, exact and continuous, so a lake or a pond is its own colour and the march runs only where the volume knows something the thickness doesn't: water that changes along the ray, a pour's silt in a pond, stirred water in a pit. With the stops and the mask edge a column at a time the shallows were 1 m squares and staircases, and marching plain sea at a metre a step met the closed form along a seam. The colour returned holds the far field's estimate of the sand under the column, faded by `through` in the caller before the framebuffer's sand is added (a colour missing its bed and divided by its alpha after the tonemap came out as saturated cyan). `through` = t × (1 − Fresnel) × (1 − foam) is the share of the pixel that is the bed already in the framebuffer (darkened to wet sand by the alpha blend, as thin water was before); the colour returned leaves that share out. Past `FarStart` (40 m) the march fades to the closed form over the column (in the texel's own water while the fragment is inside the window, else the sea's: the same two glow terms integrated exactly along the ray, then the sand under it lit through that depth), fully by `FarEnd` (56 m): the window's edge (at least 48 m from the camera) never shows, and near and far agree by construction where they meet. The thin-sheet see-through rule (`SHEET_OPAQUE`) stays for the far field and with the fog off (`gFogNear`).
- **Camera under water** (`WaterFogVolume.CameraInWater`, from `VoxelWaterSystem.WaterAt` or the sea's surface over the camera): the terrain marches from the camera to each fragment (`terrain.frag`), and so does every water surface after its own shading, which from below is the sky through the surface (no refraction yet) under the water's own glow mirrored on the underside.
- **Off** (`WaterFogVolume.Enabled`, the Terrain panel): the water body by depth alone, as before (`waterBodyDepth`), for A/B.

## Measured (2026-10-27, Release, RTX 4090, 1600×900, `--wind 14 --water-pipes`)

- **GPU** (`--bench-fog 12`, fog on against off in 2 s blocks, medians): at the start camera (55 m off the beach, 28 m up) the frame is 0.199 ms on against 0.2005 off, within the run-to-run spread. Low over the shallows (`--cam -700 6 0 0 -25`, most of the screen water within the march's reach): *Ocean surface* 0.176 against 0.149 ms, the frame 0.284 against 0.256: **+0.028 ms**. The terrain's scope is unchanged with the camera in the air (no march). `--no-fog` turns it off from the command line.
- **Look** (captures on and off at the start camera, the shallows camera and under water at `--cam -712 -2 0 0 5`): with the final gains the sea on reads as the depth shading did, a little lighter turquoise over the first metres of the beach, the same dark blue past them; the swash shows the sand through it. Under water the sand is seen about 6 m, the surface from below is the sky through Snell's window (the ray refracted out; past the critical angle the underside mirrors the water's own glow). Before calibration the first build was milky cyan (one gain for both the shallows and the deep), then the deep went black; the bed-lit term fixed both.
- **The bed along the eye's ray** (2026-10-29, `--bench-fog 12`, A B A B, A the same build with the `waterSceneBehind` lookup taken out): at the crater lake's west shore (`--weather-offset 725 --cam -175 76 0 0 -10`) the fog's cost in *Voxel water* went from 0.0031, 0.0041 ms to 0.0051, 0.0061 and in the frame from 0.0043, 0.0042 to 0.0060, 0.0060: **+0.002 ms**. Low over the sea's shallows (`--wind 14 --cam -700 6 0 0 -25`) the *Ocean surface* delta was 0.0133, 0.0113 before and 0.0154, 0.0113 after, inside the run-to-run spread. *Reflection copy* unchanged (0.0113: with the reflections on the depth was copied already). Looked at the lake (still water, frames match): the shelf unchanged, the water past its edge bluer and deeper where the eye looks down to the deep bed, no line at the edge.
- **Fill** (the `fog fill` line the game prints on exit; sections timed once): a bathymetry block costs **0.12 ms** on the main thread (mask 0.07, ground corners 0.004, columns 0.006, cells 0.04, copy 0.002), keying the window's 48 blocks **0.5 ms** once per bathymetry rebuild; about 105 blocks are filled over a 12 s bench at the shore (the first window, then the blocks the streaming's rebuilds change). With the 0.5 ms budget a frame that keys and fills runs about 0.6 ms; the multi-millisecond peaks the first runs logged were the first call's JIT and GC pauses landing inside the timer, not the fill (its sections sum to the 0.12). The first version read the sea mask per column with a 7 × 7 scan and the ground with four grid reads per column, and keyed every block on the grid's version, so each of the streaming's rebuilds refilled all 48 blocks at 3 ms a frame for 6 frames.

## Not yet, and known gaps

- **The swell above the volume's sea:** sea columns without a sim block are filled to the sea-level layer; from the air the crests take the closed form down to it, but from under water the band between the real surface and that layer is wrong by the wave height, and the surface seen from below speckles along it (each pixel's sample lands in water or air by its jitter).
- **Solid texels are a metre coarse:** the from-surface march now ends at the bed the fragment knows (its thickness, continuous from the depth map or the mesh), with the volume's solid texels only as the stop for what the thickness can't know (a cave roof, a dug pit's wall); before, the stop came a column at a time and drew the shallows as 1 m squares. Under water the terrain's own fragment is the stop. Fog density is still read per metre.
- **Roofs over the sea in bathymetry-filled blocks** count as ground down to the sand (the grid holds one top per column); the sim's blocks, which know the voxels, cover anywhere a march starts.
- **No caustics**, and the bed is not displaced by the refraction (the framebuffer has no colour buffer to look up); spray and rain are not fogged under water.
- **The automaton** does not carry colour.
